# Operational Framework Roadmap

## Purpose

This roadmap describes how the family of Emerald operational frameworks may evolve across hospitality industries.

It is a strategic sequence, not a delivery schedule or commitment. Progress to a new phase should follow evidence that the preceding Framework is useful, teachable, and effective in real operations.

## One Core Platform, Distinct Industry Frameworks

Every hospitality industry has operating realities that deserve their own Framework.

Beach Clubs, Restaurants, Resorts, Hotels, and Events should not be forced into one generic operating manual. Each industry Framework should define its own guest journey, Roles, workflows, playbooks, knowledge, decisions, and standards.

The Frameworks may share enduring principles and selected business concepts. The software may share one Core Platform for stable capabilities such as identity, authorization, operational history, coordination, and trustworthy information. Shared foundations must not erase meaningful differences between industries.

The intended relationship is:

- **One Core Platform** provides dependable common product foundations.
- **One Framework for each industry** defines how outstanding operations work in that setting.
- **Venue-specific operating choices** adapt the relevant Framework without silently becoming universal rules.

Commonality should be proven through real use. It should not be assumed merely because two industries use similar words.

## Phase 1 — Beach Clubs

### Focus

Define outstanding operation for beach clubs and organized beach service.

### Operational context

Beach clubs combine distributed Service Locations, outdoor conditions, long staff movement, sunbed and table service, dynamic Zones, preparation destinations, changing guest locations, and intense seasonal pressure.

### Framework priorities

- guest arrival, seating, and official service opening;
- clear responsibility for each active Customer Group;
- Service Location organization and transfer;
- repeated Order Rounds and preparation coordination;
- outdoor communication and operational continuity;
- Billing and Payment coordination without distracting from service;
- shift handovers, exception handling, and coaching;
- standards for clarity, pace, teamwork, and guest care.

### Evidence required before expansion

The Beach Operations Framework should be used and reviewed by experienced beach club operators. Its workflows should improve guest experience, team performance, or owner confidence without depending on Emerald software to be understood.

## Phase 2 — Restaurants

### Focus

Create a Restaurant Operations Framework informed by restaurant service rather than treating it as a minor variation of beach operations.

### Operational context

Restaurants may introduce reservations, courses, indoor table turns, kitchen pacing, host coordination, bill splitting, delivery of multiple courses, and different relationships between dining room and preparation teams.

### Framework priorities

- arrival, reservation, and table flow;
- dining-room responsibility and teamwork;
- course timing and kitchen coordination;
- table-turn quality without rushing hospitality;
- exceptions, allergies, special requests, and recovery;
- closing, handover, and service review.

### Relationship to the Core Platform

The Restaurant Framework may share Customer Group, Order, Bill, Payment, Capability, and Activity Log concepts where their meanings remain valid. Restaurant-specific operating concepts must be defined deliberately rather than forced into beach terminology.

## Phase 3 — Resorts

### Focus

Create a Resort Operations Framework for hospitality delivered across several connected service environments.

### Operational context

Resorts may combine pools, beaches, restaurants, bars, rooms, activities, guest entitlements, and service that continues across locations and times.

### Framework priorities

- coordination between distinct service outlets;
- guest recognition without unnecessary personal information;
- handovers across teams and operating periods;
- room, package, entitlement, and Payment relationships;
- service requests that move between departments;
- Organization and Venue oversight with clear local responsibility.

### Relationship to earlier Frameworks

The Resort Framework may use approved parts of the Beach Club and Restaurant Frameworks as modules. It should not duplicate them or merge them into one unclear universal workflow.

## Phase 4 — Hotels

### Focus

Create a Hotel Operations Framework for the continuous guest stay and the coordination of front office, rooms, service, and supporting departments.

### Operational context

Hotels operate across longer guest journeys, around-the-clock handovers, room readiness, housekeeping, maintenance, requests, incidents, and several points of service.

### Framework priorities

- arrival, stay, and departure continuity;
- room status and readiness;
- cross-department requests and ownership;
- shift and overnight handovers;
- service recovery and escalation;
- guest privacy, dignity, and appropriate information access;
- dependable operational awareness across the property.

### Relationship to the Core Platform

The Core Platform should support shared identity, responsibility, coordination, history, and decision guidance only where meanings are genuinely common. Hotel-specific concepts must belong to the Hotel Framework.

## Phase 5 — Events

### Focus

Create an Event Operations Framework for temporary, high-coordination hospitality operations.

### Operational context

Events may involve short setup periods, temporary layouts, high guest volume, external partners, strict timing, changing responsibilities, and rapid transition from preparation to live service and close-down.

### Framework priorities

- pre-event planning and readiness;
- temporary Zones, Service Locations, and teams;
- briefing and role clarity;
- timed service coordination;
- incident and exception response;
- supplier and partner handoffs;
- close-down, reconciliation, and learning after the event.

### Relationship to other Frameworks

Event operations may occur within beach clubs, restaurants, resorts, or hotels. The Event Framework should complement the host industry Framework and define only the additional temporary operating needs.

## Evolution Rules

The Framework family should evolve according to these rules:

1. Learn from real operators before defining a standard.
2. Keep each industry’s operational language and needs explicit.
3. Share a principle or concept only when its meaning is genuinely stable.
4. Preserve one official name for each shared business concept.
5. Do not turn one client’s preference into a universal standard without evidence.
6. Keep Framework guidance useful without software.
7. Let software support approved Frameworks; do not let existing software limits define them.
8. Record material product and business decisions before implementation.
9. Test every phase through observable guest, team, and owner outcomes.
10. Expand only when quality and operational trust can be preserved.

## Long-Term Direction

The long-term aim is not one generic framework for all hospitality.

It is a coherent family of industry Frameworks, each capable of improving operations on its own, supported by a shared Core Platform that makes excellent practice easier to execute consistently.
