# Host Operational Playbook

## Scope and authority

This playbook defines how the Host contributes to Beach Club operations. It is written for staff training, operational review, and the design of BeachOS support.

It must be read with:

- `framework/FRAMEWORK_PRINCIPLES.md`;
- `framework/04 Workflows/VENUE_OPENING_WORKFLOW.md`;
- accepted decisions DEC-030 through DEC-039;
- the Domain Model and Project Glossary.

In this playbook, **BeachOS** means the software supporting the Beach Operations Framework. The name does not change the approved product naming or architecture.

The Host Role describes responsibility. It does not grant authority by itself. Protected actions require the relevant Capability and must satisfy every business rule (DEC-038). The Host does not officially open a Customer Group; the Responsible Waiter does (DEC-035).

# 1. Mission

The Host exists to create a calm, informed connection between arriving guests and the live Venue operation. Success means that guests are welcomed promptly, guided honestly, and seated thoughtfully; that the team understands arrival pressure and changing needs; that operational problems reach the right responsible person early; and that continuity is preserved through opening, service, handover, and closing. The Host coordinates the experience without taking over the specialist responsibilities of other departments.

# 2. Responsibilities

## Before service

- Understand the expected operating context for the Business Date.
- Confirm which Operational Departments are mandatory for opening.
- Confirm who is responsible for each department’s readiness declaration.
- Coordinate the Venue Opening Workflow.
- Follow up on missing, NOT READY, and BLOCKED declarations.
- Escalate unresolved blockers to the appropriate responsible person or Manager.
- Open the Venue only when every mandatory department is READY and the Host holds the required Capability.
- Preserve the distinction between coordinating readiness and personally verifying departmental work.

## During guest arrival

- Notice arriving guests promptly.
- Welcome guests and establish the immediate human connection.
- Understand the party’s practical needs through respectful conversation.
- Explain availability and waiting honestly.
- Identify suitable available Service Locations using current operational information and human judgment.
- Avoid making promises that the operation cannot keep.
- Pass responsibility clearly to the Responsible Waiter.

## During seating

- Propose an appropriate Service Location.
- Confirm that the location is shown as available before escorting the party.
- Escort guests and explain the next service step.
- Make the handoff to the Responsible Waiter clear.
- Never treat seating, NFC identification, or manual location lookup as Customer Group opening.
- Never create a Bill, charge, Order, transfer, or Payment through the seating action.

## During active service

- Maintain awareness of arrival pressure, Service Location availability, visible preparation delays, and guest-facing disruption.
- Coordinate waiting guests fairly and honestly.
- Recognize when guests need attention without pretending to own preparation, Billing, or service responsibility.
- Route operational issues to the person or department that owns them.
- Support guest transfers without bypassing the approved transfer workflow.
- Keep affected guests informed using confirmed information.
- Escalate safety, equipment, weather, service, or guest-experience risks promptly.

## During handover

- Transfer all unresolved arrival, waiting, seating, guest-care, and operational context to the incoming Host or responsible leader.
- Identify what is confirmed, what is pending, and what requires a decision.
- Do not rely on personal memory as the only source of continuity.
- Confirm that the receiving person understands urgent priorities.

## At the end of service

- Stop accepting new arrival commitments when the Venue’s approved closing direction requires it.
- Resolve or hand over every waiting or unseated party.
- Confirm that unresolved guest-facing issues have an owner.
- Share useful closing context with the Manager and relevant departments.
- Contribute observations for coaching and operational improvement.
- Do not declare operational or financial work complete on behalf of another Role.

# 3. Daily Operational Timeline

## Before arrival

### Objective

Build a clear picture of the coming service before guests arrive.

### Inputs

- Business Date.
- Expected Venue operating conditions.
- Mandatory Operational Departments.
- Department responsible people.
- Current Zone and Service Location arrangement.
- Confirmed Service Location availability.
- Approved operating instructions and known constraints.
- Human briefing from the Manager and team.

### Actions

1. Arrive with enough time to understand the day before guest-facing pressure begins.
2. Confirm which Host is coordinating opening.
3. Review the physical Venue and staff briefing at the level needed for guest coordination.
4. Confirm the mandatory departments and their responsible people.
5. Understand the current Service Location and Zone arrangement.
6. Ask the Manager about known constraints, events, unavailable locations, or safety concerns.
7. Keep unconfirmed expectations separate from confirmed operational facts.
8. Prepare a manual continuity method if BeachOS or connectivity is unavailable.

### Expected Outcome

The Host understands the operating context, knows who owns each readiness declaration, and can begin the opening workflow without relying on assumptions.

## Venue Opening

### Objective

Open the Venue only after every mandatory Operational Department has confirmed readiness.

### Inputs

- Opening Dashboard.
- Mandatory department list.
- Responsible person for each department.
- READY, NOT READY, or BLOCKED declarations.
- Mandatory reasons for NOT READY and BLOCKED.
- Delays, blocking issues, and comments.
- Host’s required Capability.

### Actions

1. Monitor declarations from every mandatory department.
2. Follow up on missing declarations.
3. Ask for a clear reason when a department is NOT READY or BLOCKED.
4. Connect dependent departments when one blocker affects another.
5. Escalate issues that require leadership, safety judgment, maintenance, or another owner.
6. Do not mark a department READY on its behalf.
7. Do not open because the expected opening time has arrived.
8. When every mandatory department is READY, review the complete picture.
9. Intentionally execute OPEN VENUE when authorized.
10. Confirm that the Opening Report exists and the Venue is officially open.

### Expected Outcome

The Business Date has an official operational start, guest arrivals are allowed, and the permanent Opening Report accurately records the opening conditions.

## Guest Arrival

### Objective

Make every arriving party feel noticed, welcomed, and honestly informed.

### Inputs

- Confirmation that the Venue is open.
- Current Service Location availability.
- Current waiting situation.
- Party size and needs shared directly by the guests.
- Approved operating constraints.
- Human observation of the arrival area.

### Actions

1. Acknowledge the arriving party as soon as practical.
2. Welcome the guests calmly.
3. Ask only the questions needed to guide the next step.
4. Understand party size, accessibility needs, children or family needs, and strong preferences without making assumptions.
5. Check current availability.
6. Explain the immediate next step and any honest uncertainty.
7. Seat the party, place it into the manual waiting process, or escalate an unusual need.
8. Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.

### Expected Outcome

The guests know they have been recognized, understand what will happen next, and are not given a promise based on outdated or assumed availability.

## Guest Seating

### Objective

Guide the party to a suitable available Service Location and create a clear handoff to service.

### Inputs

- Party needs and preferences.
- Available Service Locations.
- Physical Area, location type, and accessibility context.
- Current operational Zones.
- Confirmed unavailable or occupied locations.
- Responsible Waiter arrangement.

### Actions

1. Select a suitable option through human judgment using confirmed availability.
2. Explain meaningful choices honestly when more than one suitable option exists.
3. Confirm the Service Location is still available before escorting the party.
4. Escort the guests to the location.
5. Identify the location through NFC or manual lookup when useful.
6. Make clear which Responsible Waiter will take responsibility.
7. Hand the party over to the Responsible Waiter.
8. Allow the Responsible Waiter to intentionally open the Customer Group.

### Expected Outcome

The party is at the correct available Service Location, understands the next service step, and has a clear service handoff. No Customer Group or financial action occurs merely because the Host seated the party or identified the location.

## Waiting List Management

### Objective

Treat waiting guests fairly, honestly, and personally when immediate seating is unavailable.

### Inputs

- Current physical queue or manually maintained waiting information.
- Confirmed Service Location availability.
- Party size and practical needs.
- Human estimates from the operation.
- Guest contact preference only where an approved local process allows it.

### Actions

1. Explain that the Venue is currently full or lacks a suitable available location.
2. Give an honest estimate only when the operation can support one.
3. Avoid presenting an estimate as a guarantee.
4. Maintain the approved manual waiting order fairly.
5. Reassess when a suitable Service Location becomes available.
6. Keep guests informed when the estimate materially changes.
7. Escalate conflict, accessibility concerns, or exceptional guest needs.
8. Avoid creating unofficial reservations or hidden priority rules.

### Expected Outcome

Waiting guests understand their position and options, promises remain realistic, and available Service Locations are assigned through a fair, visible operating practice.

> **Current product boundary:** Waiting-list management is not an accepted first-version BeachOS feature. Until separately approved, the Host follows the Venue’s manual process. BeachOS may show current Service Location state but must not be described as holding or sequencing a waiting list.

## VIP Recognition

### Objective

Provide appropriate recognition without compromising fairness, privacy, or other guests’ experience.

### Inputs

- Direct human recognition.
- A current Manager briefing.
- An approved Venue instruction.
- The guest’s stated needs.

### Actions

1. Welcome the guest naturally.
2. Confirm identity discreetly when special handling depends on it.
3. Follow only approved hospitality instructions.
4. Do not promise unavailable Service Locations or bypass safety and operational rules.
5. Involve the Manager when a special request affects priority, pricing, privacy, or another guest.
6. Keep unnecessary personal details out of operational notes and conversation.
7. Ensure recognition feels hospitable rather than theatrical or intrusive.

### Expected Outcome

The guest receives thoughtful, discreet service without unapproved privilege, unnecessary personal data, or disruption to operational trust.

> **Current product boundary:** Customer profiles, loyalty, and VIP recognition are not accepted first-version BeachOS features. Recognition remains human and based on approved Venue practice. BeachOS must not infer or label a guest as VIP.

## Guest Transfers

### Objective

Help a Customer Group move safely without losing service continuity or creating location confusion.

### Inputs

- Guest request or operational need.
- Current Customer Group and Service Location.
- Proposed destination Service Location.
- Confirmed destination availability.
- Responsible Waiter.
- Staff Member holding Transfer Customer Group, where the Host does not hold it.
- Approved transfer and charging rules.

### Actions

1. Understand why the guests want or need to move.
2. Check whether the proposed destination is currently available.
3. Explain any confirmed operational limitation without inventing a financial answer.
4. Coordinate with the Responsible Waiter.
5. Ask an authorized Staff Member to perform the transfer, or perform it only if the Host holds the required Capability and the transfer is allowed.
6. Wait for confirmed transfer success before telling guests that the move is complete.
7. Escort or guide the guests to the destination.
8. Confirm that the previous location is released and the new location is shown correctly.
9. Escalate unresolved charging questions rather than guessing.

### Expected Outcome

The complete Customer Group moves as one preserved operational context, the destination is correct, the old location is released, and the guests experience continuity.

## Operational Monitoring

### Objective

Maintain enough situational awareness to protect the guest journey without taking over other Roles.

### Inputs

- Physical observation.
- Current Service Location and Customer Group states visible to authorized staff.
- Responsible Waiter information.
- Bar and Kitchen Ticket states.
- Open Bills visible only where the Host has an approved business need and Capability.
- Opening Report and known opening issues.
- Direct updates from departments.

### Actions

1. Observe guest arrival, waiting, movement, and visible service friction.
2. Use confirmed system state to test assumptions.
3. Notice mismatches between the digital view and the physical operation.
4. Ask the responsible person to clarify or correct the source business action.
5. Watch for patterns such as repeated waiting, unclear responsibility, or visible preparation delay.
6. Route the concern to the Role that owns the work.
7. Keep guests informed when the issue affects them and confirmed information is available.
8. Do not alter Bills, Orders, Tickets, or responsibility merely to make a dashboard look better.

### Expected Outcome

Operational uncertainty is found early, responsible people remain accountable for their work, and guest-facing effects receive timely human attention.

## Issue Escalation

### Objective

Move an issue quickly to the person able and authorized to resolve it while protecting guests and operational truth.

### Inputs

- Direct guest report.
- Staff report.
- Physical observation.
- Confirmed BeachOS state.
- Severity, urgency, safety impact, and affected operational area.
- Approved Venue escalation practice.

### Actions

1. Listen and establish the immediate facts.
2. Protect safety first.
3. Acknowledge the issue without assigning blame.
4. Identify the responsible department or leader.
5. Escalate with a short statement of what happened, who or what is affected, urgency, and what has already been done.
6. Stay connected to the guest-facing outcome.
7. Record information only through approved existing history or manual processes.
8. Confirm ownership of the next action.
9. Follow up until the guest or operation has a clear outcome or handover.

### Expected Outcome

The issue has an accountable owner, urgent risk is controlled, affected guests are not abandoned, and confirmed facts remain distinguishable from assumptions.

> **Current product boundary:** A general incident or complaint-management module has not been accepted. BeachOS may expose existing operational facts and Activity Logs, but this playbook does not promise issue tickets, automated escalation, or complaint records.

## Shift Handover

### Objective

Transfer situational awareness and unresolved responsibility without losing context.

### Inputs

- Current waiting parties managed through the approved manual process.
- Unresolved guest concerns.
- Expected arrivals known through approved Venue practice.
- Current Service Location availability.
- Transfers or seating decisions still in progress.
- Operational blockers and department updates.
- Confirmed BeachOS state and relevant manual notes.

### Actions

1. Meet the incoming Host or responsible leader directly.
2. Separate urgent items from background information.
3. For each unresolved matter, state the situation, confirmed facts, owner, next action, and promised follow-up.
4. Identify any information that exists only in the manual process.
5. Confirm the incoming person understands immediate priorities.
6. Notify affected departments when responsibility changes.
7. Do not end the handover with an unowned guest-facing issue.

### Expected Outcome

The incoming person can continue the operation without reconstructing it from memory, and every unresolved matter has clear ownership.

## Venue Closing

### Objective

End the Host’s operational period without creating abandoned guests, hidden commitments, or lost knowledge.

### Inputs

- Manager’s approved closing direction.
- Current arrivals and manual waiting situation.
- Occupied Service Locations and active Customer Groups.
- Open guest-facing issues.
- Current operational and preparation state.
- Incoming shift information, where the Venue remains open.

### Actions

1. Confirm whether this is a personal shift handover or the Venue’s end of guest intake.
2. Stop making new arrival or seating commitments when directed through the approved Venue process.
3. Resolve or explicitly hand over every waiting party.
4. Confirm that seated guests remain owned by their Responsible Waiter.
5. Escalate unresolved complaints, transfers, safety issues, or service delays.
6. Share useful observations about recurring pressure or confusion.
7. Do not close Bills, Customer Groups, Tickets, or the Venue on behalf of another Role unless a separately approved Capability and rule allow it.
8. Preserve relevant operational history through approved processes.

### Expected Outcome

No guest-facing commitment is abandoned, active service ownership remains clear, and the next shift or Manager receives the context needed for continuity.

> **Current product boundary:** A formal daily Venue Closing Workflow has not yet been approved. BeachOS support is limited to accepted Customer Group, Ticket, Bill, Payment, Activity Log, and current-state visibility. This section defines Host conduct, not a new software closing action.

# 4. Decision Tree

## Venue full

### Situation

No suitable Service Location is currently shown and confirmed as available.

### Decision

Tell the guests honestly that immediate seating is unavailable. Decide whether to offer the Venue’s approved manual waiting process, suggest another suitable option, or explain that service cannot be promised.

### Escalation

Escalate disputes, accessibility needs, unclear availability, or pressure to bypass the waiting order to the Manager. Do not treat an occupied or unavailable location as available.

### Software assistance

BeachOS can show current Service Location states and the active Customer Group associated with an occupied location, subject to authorization. Waiting-list sequencing and availability prediction are not approved features.

## Family arrives

### Situation

A party arrives with children or identifies family-related practical needs.

### Decision

Ask what the family needs rather than assuming. Choose among confirmed available Service Locations using physical suitability, safety, comfort, and guest preference.

### Escalation

Escalate accessibility, safety, capacity, equipment, or accommodation questions that are outside the Host’s approved judgment.

### Software assistance

BeachOS can show location identity, type, Area, availability, and current operational context already held by the system. Capacity, accessibility matching, and family recommendations are future considerations and must not be claimed as current intelligence.

## VIP arrives

### Situation

The Host recognizes a guest or receives an approved Manager briefing that special hospitality attention is appropriate.

### Decision

Welcome the guest discreetly and apply only approved human instructions. Balance recognition with availability, fairness, safety, privacy, and the experience of other guests.

### Escalation

Escalate requests involving priority, price, privacy, unavailable locations, exceptions, or commitments outside the Host’s authority.

### Software assistance

BeachOS currently provides no approved VIP profile, ranking, recognition, or recommendation. The software must not infer VIP status or expose unnecessary personal information.

## Kitchen delayed

### Situation

Kitchen Tickets or direct department information show that preparation is delayed and guests may be affected.

### Decision

Confirm the delay with the Kitchen’s responsible person, identify which guest journeys may be affected, and coordinate honest communication through the Responsible Waiter. Do not invent a preparation time.

### Escalation

Escalate a sustained or severe delay to the Kitchen lead and Manager, particularly when it affects many Customer Groups or creates safety or service-recovery needs.

### Software assistance

BeachOS can show confirmed Kitchen Ticket states, submission times, Service Location context, and status changes already approved in the product vision. Preparation estimates, delay prediction, and automated recovery advice are future intelligence.

## Complaint received

### Situation

A guest expresses dissatisfaction directly to the Host.

### Decision

Listen without interruption, acknowledge the experience, establish the immediate facts, and decide what human response will help the guest now. Do not argue, promise an unauthorized remedy, or blame another Staff Member.

### Escalation

Escalate safety, harassment, financial disputes, refund requests, serious service failure, or any remedy outside the Host’s authority to the appropriate Manager or authorized Role.

### Software assistance

BeachOS can provide relevant confirmed context such as Service Location, Responsible Waiter, Order, Ticket, Bill, transfer, and Activity Log history when the Host is authorized to see it. Complaint case management and suggested compensation are not approved features.

## Service delay

### Situation

A guest appears to be waiting longer than expected for attention, preparation, delivery, or another service step.

### Decision

Check the physical situation and confirmed state, identify the responsible person, and ensure the guest receives an honest update. Decide whether immediate human attention is needed before the underlying work completes.

### Escalation

Escalate when responsibility is unclear, the delay is repeated, the guest is distressed, or several Customer Groups are affected.

### Software assistance

BeachOS can show existing timestamps, Ticket states, Responsible Waiter, and location context. Automated service-delay detection, thresholds, and recommendations require future approval.

## Bad weather

### Situation

Weather threatens guest comfort, safe movement, equipment, or continued outdoor operation.

### Decision

Follow the Venue’s approved safety direction, communicate calmly, and help coordinate guest movement or service changes. Human leaders decide whether service is restricted, relocated, paused, or stopped.

### Escalation

Escalate immediately to the Manager and relevant safety, Beach Operations, or Maintenance responsibility. Safety takes priority over seating preference and administrative convenience.

### Software assistance

No weather integration, forecasting, alerting, or automated response is currently approved. BeachOS may show existing Service Locations and Customer Groups to authorized staff, but weather decisions remain human.

## Equipment failure

### Situation

An equipment failure affects readiness, service, safety, preparation, Payment, or operational continuity.

### Decision

Protect people first, confirm the affected department, and avoid promising normal service until the responsible person explains the impact. During opening, the department declares NOT READY or BLOCKED with a reason. After opening, coordinate the approved manual response.

### Escalation

Escalate to the responsible department, Maintenance, and Manager according to severity. Do not permit the Venue to open while a mandatory department remains NOT READY or BLOCKED.

### Software assistance

During opening, BeachOS can show the department status, required reason, comments, and Opening Dashboard state under DEC-039. A general maintenance system, equipment monitoring, or automated workaround recommendation is not currently approved.

# 5. BeachOS Support

## Operating boundary

BeachOS supports the Host by presenting confirmed information, enforcing accepted rules, preserving history, and—where separately approved—offering explainable recommendations.

**The software advises. The Host always decides when human judgment is required, within approved rules.**

Advice must never be presented as a decision already made. A recommendation must explain the confirmed information on which it depends and must remain distinguishable from a mandatory business rule.

The Host cannot decide to bypass an accepted invariant. For example:

- BeachOS must not enable OPEN VENUE until every mandatory Operational Department is READY (DEC-039).
- The Host cannot officially open a Customer Group (DEC-035).
- NFC identifies a Service Location but performs no operational or financial action (DEC-030).
- A protected action requires the relevant Capability; the Role alone is insufficient (DEC-038).

## Recommendation standard

Any future BeachOS recommendation for the Host must:

- use confirmed, relevant information;
- identify uncertainty and missing information;
- explain why the recommendation is being shown;
- distinguish advice from a mandatory rule;
- avoid hidden use of sensitive personal information;
- allow the Host to reject or adapt the advice where business rules permit;
- preserve accountability for the human decision;
- never claim emotional, safety, or guest-context understanding that the software does not have.

# 6. KPIs

KPIs are signals for learning, not a complete judgment of the Host. They must be interpreted with guest volume, staffing, weather, equipment, layout, department readiness, and other operating conditions.

## Average guest waiting time

**Definition:** Average elapsed time from a party entering the approved waiting process until it is seated or leaves that process.

**Purpose:** Shows whether the Venue communicates and moves waiting demand effectively.

**Caution:** Waiting-list tracking is not currently an approved BeachOS feature. This measure requires a reliable approved process before automation.

## Greeting time

**Definition:** Time from a guest party’s observable arrival to its first meaningful acknowledgment by the Host or assigned arrival responsibility.

**Purpose:** Measures whether guests feel noticed promptly.

**Caution:** Automated arrival detection is not approved. Manual observation may be used for coaching samples, not covert surveillance.

## Seating time

**Definition:** Time from the start of the seating decision to the party reaching the selected Service Location and receiving a clear handoff.

**Purpose:** Identifies avoidable searching, unclear availability, or weak coordination.

**Caution:** Faster is not always better. Accessibility needs, guest choice, and good hospitality may appropriately take longer.

## Opening readiness completion

**Definition:** Percentage of mandatory Operational Departments with a valid declaration before the target opening time, considered alongside the actual opening outcome.

**Purpose:** Shows whether opening responsibility and preparation are clear.

**Caution:** It must never reward false READY declarations or pressure departments to hide blockers.

## Opening delay

**Definition:** Difference between the planned opening time and official Venue opening time, with the reasons preserved in the Opening Report.

**Purpose:** Identifies recurring blockers and dependencies.

**Caution:** A safe, truthful delay is better than opening while unready.

## Open operational issues

**Definition:** Number of guest-facing or continuity issues known to the Host that do not yet have a confirmed resolution or handover.

**Purpose:** Highlights unowned or unresolved work.

**Caution:** A general issue-management feature is not approved. This remains an operational measure until its business lifecycle is defined.

## Issue resolution time

**Definition:** Time from confirmed recognition of an operational issue to resolution or an accepted handover to the correct owner.

**Purpose:** Shows how effectively the operation responds to disruption.

**Caution:** Severity and quality of resolution matter more than speed alone.

## Handover completeness

**Definition:** Percentage of unresolved matters that have a stated situation, owner, next action, and acknowledged receiver at handover.

**Purpose:** Protects Operational Continuity.

**Caution:** This should assess the process, not encourage unnecessary recording of staff activity.

## Guest satisfaction indicators

**Definition:** A balanced collection of direct guest feedback, complaint patterns, service-recovery outcomes, and observed guest experience.

**Purpose:** Keeps hospitality—not internal administration—as the final outcome.

**Caution:** No single score fully represents guest experience. Customer profiles, automated sentiment analysis, and satisfaction scoring are not approved product features.

## Availability accuracy

**Definition:** Frequency with which the Service Location state used for seating matches the real physical situation.

**Purpose:** Measures trust in the operational mirror.

**Caution:** A mismatch should trigger investigation of workflow, timing, and system state rather than automatic blame.

## KPI use rules

- Use trends and context, not isolated numbers.
- Never reward unsafe opening, false declarations, hidden complaints, or rushed guests.
- Compare the process with its operating conditions.
- Use evidence first for coaching and system improvement.
- Do not infer intention from timing data alone.
- Do not create a KPI merely because BeachOS can count something.

# 7. Common Mistakes

## Trying to personally verify every department

**Why it happens:** The Host feels responsible for the complete opening and confuses coordination with specialist verification.

**Risk:** Department ownership becomes unclear and the Host becomes a bottleneck.

**BeachOS prevention:** The Opening Dashboard shows the responsible person and declaration for each department. The Host follows up but does not replace the declaration.

## Opening because the scheduled time has arrived

**Why it happens:** Pressure to avoid a visible delay.

**Risk:** Guests enter an operation that is not ready.

**BeachOS prevention:** OPEN VENUE remains unavailable until all mandatory departments are READY under DEC-039.

## Marking a department READY on its behalf

**Why it happens:** The Host wants to remove a blocker quickly.

**Risk:** The Opening Report becomes untrustworthy and responsibility is hidden.

**BeachOS prevention:** Department readiness must remain attributable to its responsible person. Capability and business rules apply independently of Role.

## Seating before confirming availability

**Why it happens:** The Host relies on memory or the last observed physical state.

**Risk:** Two parties may be directed to the same Service Location or an unavailable location.

**BeachOS prevention:** Show the current central Service Location state and require the Host to confirm it before escorting the party.

## Treating seating as Customer Group opening

**Why it happens:** Physical seating and official service opening occur close together.

**Risk:** Responsibility and Beach Sunbed Financial Obligations may be created by the wrong person.

**BeachOS prevention:** Identification and seating perform no operational or financial action. Only the Responsible Waiter intentionally opens the Customer Group under DEC-035.

## Overpromising waiting time

**Why it happens:** The Host wants to reassure guests.

**Risk:** A hopeful estimate becomes a broken promise.

**BeachOS prevention:** Current BeachOS support is limited to confirmed availability. Future estimates, if approved, must show uncertainty and remain advice.

## Using unofficial VIP priority

**Why it happens:** Recognition or pressure creates an informal exception.

**Risk:** Unfairness, privacy problems, and operational disruption.

**BeachOS prevention:** BeachOS has no approved VIP ranking or automatic priority. Any exception remains a human, Manager-led decision under approved Venue policy.

## Guessing about delays

**Why it happens:** Guests want an immediate answer before the Host has confirmed the cause.

**Risk:** Conflicting information reduces trust.

**BeachOS prevention:** Show confirmed Ticket status, time, location, and responsible context. Do not present an unapproved prediction as fact.

## Taking ownership away from the responsible Role

**Why it happens:** The Host wants to solve the guest’s problem personally.

**Risk:** The real owner becomes unclear and specialist work may be changed without authority.

**BeachOS prevention:** Keep Responsible Waiter, department responsibility, and required Capabilities visible.

## Escalating without facts

**Why it happens:** Urgency leads to a vague message such as “everything is delayed.”

**Risk:** The receiving person cannot act effectively.

**BeachOS prevention:** Present confirmed context already available, while the Host adds concise human explanation of impact and urgency.

## Failing to close the communication loop

**Why it happens:** The Host passes an issue onward and assumes it is finished.

**Risk:** The guest remains uninformed and the issue becomes unowned.

**BeachOS prevention:** Existing state may show that work remains open; general issue follow-up is future operational support. The Host must currently follow up through the approved human process.

## Incomplete handover

**Why it happens:** Important context remains in memory or informal conversation.

**Risk:** Waiting guests, promises, and unresolved issues are lost between shifts.

**BeachOS prevention:** Current live state and history support factual continuity. Future handover summaries may assist, but direct human acknowledgment remains necessary.

## Using KPIs to judge without context

**Why it happens:** Numbers appear objective and easy to compare.

**Risk:** Staff hide problems, rush guests, or declare false readiness.

**BeachOS prevention:** Measures should retain context and support coaching. Automated performance judgment is outside the accepted vision.

# 8. Operational Principles

## Hospitality First

The Host begins with the guest’s experience, not the convenience of the dashboard. Information matters because it helps the Host welcome honestly, coordinate thoughtfully, and protect attention for hospitality.

## Knowledge Belongs to the Business

Opening practice, seating judgment, escalation expectations, and handover standards must not live only in one experienced Host’s memory. This playbook preserves shared knowledge that can be taught, reviewed, and improved.

## The System Advises

BeachOS should show confirmed context and, where approved in the future, explain useful recommendations. It must not disguise a guess as truth or an automated outcome as human judgment.

## Humans Decide

The Host decides how to welcome, communicate, coordinate, and escalate. The Host chooses whether to open the Venue once it is eligible. Approved business rules still define what is forbidden and which actions require Capabilities.

## Coaching Before Judging

Opening delays, missed greetings, slow seating, and incomplete handovers should first be examined for unclear responsibility, workload, missing knowledge, or poor workflow. Accountability is fair only when evidence and context are understood.

## Operational Continuity

The Host protects continuity between physical and digital context, between departments, and between shifts. No waiting guest, promise, blocker, or unresolved issue should disappear because a person or device changes.

## Guest Experience Over Administration

The Host records and checks only what has operational value or an approved accountability purpose. Screens and procedures must not pull attention away from guests without a necessary reason.

## Consistency Creates Trust

Guests and staff should receive the same clear treatment in the same situation. Meaningful exceptions must be explicit, authorized, and understandable rather than informal.

## Technology Reduces Uncertainty

BeachOS should clarify what is available, confirmed, pending, blocked, or owned. When it cannot do so, the Host uses a visible manual fallback and does not claim certainty.

## Every Workflow Must Create Operational Value

Host workflows must improve guest experience, team performance, or owner confidence. If a step improves none of these, it should be challenged.
