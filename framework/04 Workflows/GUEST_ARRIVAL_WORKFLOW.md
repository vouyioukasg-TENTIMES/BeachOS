# Guest Arrival Workflow

## Scope and authority

This workflow begins at the exact moment a guest party enters the Venue and ends when the Responsible Waiter intentionally opens the Customer Group.

It describes the operation, including human responsibility and the business facts BeachOS may support. It does not prescribe software screens.

It must remain consistent with:

- the Host Operational Playbook;
- the Venue Opening Workflow;
- the Domain Model and Project Glossary;
- accepted decisions DEC-011, DEC-030 through DEC-035, DEC-038, and DEC-039.

The Host Role describes responsibility but does not grant protected authority. Capabilities authorize protected actions (DEC-038). The Host may welcome, assess, propose, identify, escort, and coordinate. The Host does not officially open the Customer Group (DEC-035).

# 1. Purpose

Guest Arrival is one of the most important moments of the guest experience because it is the first point at which the Venue turns operational readiness into personal hospitality. In a short period, guests decide whether they feel noticed, understood, and confident that the team is organized. A good arrival creates a calm path from uncertainty to clear service responsibility. A poor arrival creates confusion before service has begun: guests may feel ignored, receive an unreliable promise, be sent to the wrong Service Location, or remain unsure who will serve them.

The purpose of this workflow is to make every arrival welcoming, honest, and operationally clear while protecting the boundary between seating a party and officially opening its Customer Group.

# 2. Operational Goal

The Guest Arrival Workflow succeeds when:

- the guest party is noticed and welcomed promptly;
- the Host understands the party’s immediate practical needs without making unnecessary assumptions;
- availability is based on current operational truth;
- waiting or seating is handled honestly and fairly;
- the selected Service Location is suitable and confirmed available;
- the party is escorted without losing coordination;
- the correct Service Location is identified through NFC or manual lookup;
- the Responsible Waiter is clearly introduced and accepts service responsibility;
- the Responsible Waiter intentionally opens exactly one Customer Group while holding the required Capability;
- any immediate Beach Sunbed Financial Obligation is confirmed and created under the approved rule;
- the guest understands what will happen next;
- the physical situation and the digital operational state match.

Speed matters, but never at the cost of a false promise, unsafe seating, unclear responsibility, duplicate occupancy, or unintended financial action.

# 3. Preconditions

Before Guest Arrival begins, all of the following must be true:

## Venue readiness

- The Venue Opening Workflow has been completed for the current Business Date.
- Every mandatory Operational Department declared READY.
- An authorized Host intentionally opened the Venue under DEC-039.
- The official operational start has been recorded in the permanent Opening Report.
- Guest arrivals are allowed.

## Host readiness

- A Host is present or an approved person has been assigned the arrival responsibility.
- The Host understands the current Venue arrangement and known operating constraints.
- The Host knows how to reach the Manager and relevant operational leaders.
- The Host understands that coordination does not replace another Role’s responsibility.
- A manual continuity method is available if BeachOS or connectivity becomes unavailable.

## Service Location readiness

- Current Service Location state is available from the central source of truth.
- The physical Venue and operational state have been checked for known mismatches.
- Unavailable, occupied, or retired Service Locations are not offered.
- The current Area, Zone, and Service Location arrangement is understood.
- NFC identification has a visible manual lookup fallback.

## Service responsibility

- The Responsible Waiter arrangement is known or can be confirmed before official Customer Group opening.
- The person who will become Responsible Waiter is eligible for that responsibility.
- The Responsible Waiter holds the Open Customer Group Capability.
- The Host understands that only the Responsible Waiter may perform the official opening action.

## Business rules

- One active Customer Group per Service Location remains enforced under DEC-011.
- NFC and manual identification perform no business or financial action under DEC-030.
- Customer Group is the primary operational entity under DEC-031.
- A Customer Group may exist without a Bill until its first approved Financial Obligation under DEC-033.
- Opening at a Beach Sunbed creates the approved immediate Beach Sunbed Financial Obligation under DEC-034.
- Role and Capability remain separate under DEC-038.

If a required precondition is not true, the Host must not improvise a false operational state. The issue must be corrected or escalated before the affected step proceeds.

# 4. Complete Workflow

## Step 1 — Guest enters

### Objective

Recognize that a new guest party has entered the Venue and bring it into the arrival process without making an operational commitment.

### Inputs

- Physical arrival of one or more guests.
- Confirmation that the Venue is officially open.
- Current conditions at the entrance or arrival point.
- Human observation by the Host or assigned arrival responsibility.

### Actions

1. Notice that a new party has entered.
2. Distinguish the arriving party from guests who are already seated, waiting, departing, or moving inside the Venue.
3. Move attention toward the party as soon as practical.
4. If temporarily occupied with another guest, acknowledge the new arrival and indicate that assistance is coming.
5. Do not assume party size, purpose, booking status, preferences, or identity.

### Expected Outcome

The party has entered the arrival process and is not left uncertain about whether anyone has noticed.

### Failure Cases

- The Venue has not officially opened.
- No Host or arrival responsibility is present.
- Several parties arrive at the same time and arrival order becomes unclear.
- The Host mistakes a returning or moving guest for a new arrival.
- The party enters through an unexpected access point.

When the Venue is not open, the guests must be welcomed respectfully but must not proceed into normal arrival and seating. When responsibility or order is unclear, the Host establishes it openly and fairly.

### BeachOS Support

BeachOS can confirm whether the Venue is officially open under DEC-039. Automatic guest detection, entry sensors, facial recognition, and arrival sequencing are not approved. Noticing and acknowledging the guest remain human responsibilities.

## Step 2 — Host notices guest

### Objective

Create immediate human recognition and establish clear ownership of the arrival.

### Inputs

- The visible guest party.
- Other parties currently being assisted.
- Current arrival pressure.
- The Host’s direct observation.

### Actions

1. Make eye contact where culturally and personally appropriate.
2. Offer a prompt verbal or non-verbal acknowledgment.
3. Approach the guests, or clearly indicate where they should wait for the Host.
4. If another Host takes over, make the handoff visible to the guests.
5. Avoid leaving two Hosts assuming the other person has taken responsibility.

### Expected Outcome

The guests know who is helping them, and the team has one clear owner for the next arrival step.

### Failure Cases

- The guest is not noticed.
- Two Hosts begin separate conversations with the same party.
- Each Host assumes another person is responsible.
- The Host acknowledges the party but does not return.
- Arrival pressure prevents immediate personal attention.

The Host should make ownership explicit and, when delayed, give a brief honest acknowledgment rather than disappear.

### BeachOS Support

No accepted BeachOS feature detects whether a Host has noticed a guest. Future arrival coordination or workload assistance would require a separate decision and must not replace human attention.

## Step 3 — Greeting

### Objective

Welcome the guests and create a calm, respectful beginning to the relationship.

### Inputs

- The guest party.
- Time of day and current Venue conditions.
- The Host’s direct human judgment.
- Any approved Venue greeting standard.

### Actions

1. Greet the party warmly and naturally.
2. Introduce the Host’s role when useful.
3. Ask how the Host may help.
4. Listen before directing the party.
5. Use language the guests can understand where practical.
6. Avoid leading with rules, prices, waiting time, or administrative questions unless immediately necessary.

### Expected Outcome

The guests feel welcomed and are ready to explain what they need.

### Failure Cases

- The greeting feels mechanical or rushed.
- The Host begins questioning without welcoming.
- Language differences create uncertainty.
- The guests are upset before the conversation begins.
- The Host makes assumptions based on appearance or familiarity.

The Host should slow the interaction enough to establish understanding and request help from a colleague when communication cannot be made clear.

### BeachOS Support

Greeting is a human hospitality action. BeachOS does not generate the emotional response, decide how to greet, or infer guest needs. Approved future knowledge support may provide language or service guidance, but it cannot replace human presence.

## Step 4 — Needs assessment

### Objective

Understand the minimum practical information needed to guide the party toward suitable seating or waiting.

### Inputs

- Information voluntarily provided by the guests.
- Party size.
- Guest preferences.
- Children or family needs.
- Accessibility, mobility, shade, proximity, or other practical needs.
- Current physical conditions.
- Approved Venue operating constraints.

### Actions

1. Ask how many people are in the party.
2. Ask which type of experience or Area they prefer when a real choice exists.
3. Invite guests to state accessibility or practical needs.
4. Clarify essential requests without collecting unnecessary personal information.
5. Repeat important needs back when confirmation will prevent misunderstanding.
6. Separate a preference from a requirement.
7. Do not promise a specific Service Location before checking current availability.

### Expected Outcome

The Host understands enough to evaluate suitable options without unnecessary questioning or unsupported assumptions.

### Failure Cases

- Party size is misunderstood.
- The Host assumes what a family, older guest, disabled guest, or familiar guest needs.
- A preference is treated as guaranteed.
- Important accessibility or safety information is missed.
- Too much personal information is requested.
- The guests ask for an arrangement whose safety, capacity, or business rule is unclear.

The Host clarifies respectfully and escalates unresolved safety, capacity, or exception questions before offering a location.

### BeachOS Support

BeachOS may provide approved Service Location facts such as location type, Area, availability, and other information already defined for the location. Capacity and accessibility matching, guest profiles, preference history, and personalized recommendations are not accepted current features.

## Step 5 — Availability check

### Objective

Establish which suitable Service Locations are currently eligible to receive the party.

### Inputs

- Confirmed Service Location state from the central system.
- Physical observation.
- Party needs and preferences.
- Service Location type and Area.
- Current Zone context.
- Known unavailable or retired locations.
- Active Customer Groups.

### Actions

1. Check current availability using the central operational truth.
2. Compare the digital state with the visible physical situation when practical.
3. Exclude occupied, unavailable, retired, unsafe, or unsuitable locations.
4. Identify one or more suitable available choices.
5. Resolve or escalate any mismatch before making a promise.
6. Recheck availability if meaningful time passes before seating.
7. Do not treat an empty-looking location as available when the operational state says otherwise.

### Expected Outcome

The Host has a current, trustworthy set of suitable available options or knows that no suitable location is presently available.

### Failure Cases

- The digital state says available while the location is physically occupied.
- The digital state says occupied while the location appears empty.
- Another Staff Member begins using the location during the check.
- The location becomes unavailable.
- No option meets a safety or accessibility requirement.
- Connectivity prevents access to current central state.

The Host pauses the promise, identifies the responsible person, and resolves the mismatch through the approved operational or manual fallback process. Uncertainty must not be presented as availability.

### BeachOS Support

BeachOS maintains the central Service Location state and protects the rule that one location may have at most one active Customer Group (DEC-011). It can provide Area, Zone, identity, availability, and active Customer Group context to authorized staff. It does not replace physical awareness or human suitability judgment.

## Step 6 — Waiting or seating decision

### Objective

Choose the next honest operational path: proceed to seating or offer the approved manual waiting process.

### Inputs

- Suitable confirmed availability.
- Party needs and preferences.
- Current manual waiting situation.
- Known operating constraints.
- Human assessment of what the Venue can realistically offer.

### Actions

1. If a suitable Service Location is available, propose it clearly.
2. If more than one suitable option exists, explain only the choices that matter.
3. If no suitable location is available, explain the situation honestly.
4. Offer the approved manual waiting process where one exists.
5. Give an estimate only when supported by current human operational knowledge.
6. State uncertainty and avoid presenting an estimate as a guarantee.
7. Allow the guests to accept seating, wait, choose another option, or leave.
8. Avoid hidden priority, unofficial reservation, or unapproved exception.

### Expected Outcome

The guests make an informed choice and understand whether they will be seated, wait, select another option, or leave.

### Failure Cases

- The Venue is full.
- Only unsuitable locations are available.
- The guests reject the offered location.
- The guests expect a guaranteed waiting time.
- Another party is already waiting for the same type of location.
- Pressure is applied to bypass a fair waiting order.
- The guests leave before a decision is completed.

The Host remains honest, avoids creating a false commitment, and escalates conflict or exceptional needs to the Manager.

### BeachOS Support

BeachOS can provide current Service Location availability. A digital waiting list, queue order, waiting-time prediction, reservation handling, and automated priority are not accepted first-version features. Waiting remains an approved manual operational process until separately decided.

## Step 7 — Escort to Service Location

### Objective

Move the guests from the arrival point to the selected location while preserving welcome, certainty, and availability.

### Inputs

- Guest acceptance of the proposed location.
- Selected Service Location.
- Most recent confirmed availability.
- Safe physical route.
- Current Venue conditions.
- Responsible Waiter arrangement.

### Actions

1. Confirm the guests have accepted the proposed location.
2. Reconfirm availability immediately before or during the start of escort when needed.
3. Guide the party along a safe and practical route.
4. Maintain reasonable pace and awareness of children, mobility, luggage, heat, and crowding.
5. Continue simple hospitality rather than turning the escort into an administrative task.
6. Watch for a changed physical condition at the destination.
7. Stop and reassess if the location is occupied, unsafe, unavailable, or no longer suitable.

### Expected Outcome

The guests arrive safely at the correct available Service Location and remain confident that the Venue is coordinating their arrival.

### Failure Cases

- The Service Location becomes unavailable during escort.
- Another party reaches the location first.
- The physical location is unsafe or not ready.
- The guests change their mind.
- The Host escorts the party to the wrong visible name or number.
- Weather or congestion makes the route unsuitable.

The Host does not force completion. The Host explains the change, rechecks suitable availability, and either escorts the guests to another accepted option or returns to the waiting decision.

### BeachOS Support

BeachOS can provide stable Service Location identity, visible name or number, Area, Zone context, and current operational state. Route guidance, crowd detection, weather-aware escorting, and physical safety assessment are not approved features.

## Step 8 — NFC or manual Service Location identification

### Objective

Resolve the physical place to the correct enduring Service Location and retrieve its current operational context.

### Inputs

- Physical Service Location.
- Stable Service Location Digital Identity.
- NFC tag where available.
- Visible location name or number for manual fallback.
- Signed-in Staff Member.
- Central operational truth.

### Actions

1. Use NFC as the preferred initial identification method where it is available and practical.
2. Confirm that the resolved identity matches the visible physical location.
3. Use manual lookup by visible name or number when NFC is unavailable, damaged, unsupported, or uncertain.
4. Check the current state returned from the central system.
5. Stop if the resolved location is wrong, occupied, unavailable, or inconsistent with the physical situation.
6. Escalate a missing, moved, duplicated, or damaged physical marker through the approved process.
7. Do not treat identification as authorization or as an operational action.

### Expected Outcome

The physical place and the digital Service Location identity match, and current state is available for the handoff and official opening decision.

### Failure Cases

- NFC does not respond.
- The tag resolves to the wrong Service Location.
- Manual lookup identifies a different location than expected.
- The physical tag is missing, damaged, moved, or duplicated.
- The location state changed during escort.
- The Staff Member is not signed in or lacks authority for a later protected action.
- Connectivity prevents retrieval of current state.

The Host uses manual fallback where safe, never guesses the identity, and does not continue into official opening while the location remains uncertain.

### BeachOS Support

BeachOS resolves NFC or manual identification to the stable Service Location Digital Identity and retrieves current state from the central system under DEC-030. The tag contains no changing operational, financial, customer, or authorization information. Identification never signs in a Staff Member, grants a Capability, opens a Customer Group, or creates any financial action.

## Step 9 — Responsible Waiter introduction

### Objective

Create a clear human handoff from arrival coordination to accountable service.

### Inputs

- Guest party at the identified Service Location.
- Confirmed Service Location state.
- Eligible Responsible Waiter.
- Current Zone or service responsibility arrangement.
- Human availability of the Responsible Waiter.

### Actions

1. Confirm which eligible Staff Member will become Responsible Waiter.
2. Make direct contact with that person.
3. Introduce the Responsible Waiter to the guests where practical.
4. Communicate the Service Location and essential guest needs.
5. Avoid passing unnecessary personal information.
6. Ask the Responsible Waiter to confirm acceptance of the handoff.
7. Remain present until responsibility is clear when immediate departure would create uncertainty.
8. Do not perform the waiter’s official opening action.

### Expected Outcome

The guests know who is responsible for service, the Responsible Waiter understands the relevant context, and there is no gap between seating and service ownership.

### Failure Cases

- No eligible Responsible Waiter is available.
- Two waiters assume the other is responsible.
- Zone assignment and actual responsibility conflict.
- The proposed waiter cannot accept the group.
- The Host leaves before the handoff is understood.
- Sensitive or irrelevant guest information is shared.

The Host keeps responsibility visible, coordinates with the appropriate leader, and does not create the Customer Group until an eligible Responsible Waiter accepts the handoff.

### BeachOS Support

BeachOS can provide current Zone context, Staff Member responsibility, and the eventual Responsible Waiter association according to accepted rules. Automatic waiter selection, workload prediction, and handoff recommendations are future intelligence. The human handoff and acceptance remain operational responsibilities.

## Step 10 — Responsible Waiter intentionally opens Customer Group

### Objective

Officially begin service with the correct Customer Group, Service Location, Responsible Waiter, and financial consequence.

### Inputs

- Guest party at the confirmed Service Location.
- Correct Service Location identity and current availability.
- Eligible Responsible Waiter.
- Open Customer Group Capability.
- Applicable Service Location type.
- Applicable approved Beach Sunbed charge where relevant.
- Current central operational state.

### Actions

1. The Responsible Waiter confirms the correct party and Service Location.
2. The Responsible Waiter confirms acceptance of service responsibility.
3. The Responsible Waiter reviews any immediate approved Beach Sunbed Financial Obligation.
4. The Responsible Waiter intentionally performs the official opening action.
5. BeachOS verifies the Capability, Role-specific business rule, Venue-open state, Service Location availability, and one-active-group invariant.
6. If the location is a Beach Sunbed, the responsible waiter confirms the applicable charge as part of the intentional opening.
7. BeachOS records the Customer Group opening only after all required rules are satisfied.
8. The Host confirms the handoff is complete and returns attention to arrival coordination.

### Expected Outcome

Exactly one active Customer Group exists at the Service Location with exactly one Responsible Waiter. For a Beach Sunbed, the approved immediate Financial Obligation and Bill are created. At another Service Location type, the Customer Group may exist without a Bill until its first approved Financial Obligation. Official service has begun.

### Failure Cases

- The Venue is not officially open.
- The location is no longer available.
- Another active Customer Group already occupies the location.
- The Staff Member is not the Responsible Waiter.
- The Responsible Waiter lacks Open Customer Group.
- The applicable Beach Sunbed charge cannot be confirmed.
- The central system cannot safely record the opening.
- Repeated action risks creating a duplicate Customer Group.
- The Host attempts to open the Customer Group.

No Customer Group is opened unless the complete action succeeds. The operation must not show or communicate false success. The Host and Responsible Waiter resolve the specific cause or escalate it. Guests should receive a calm explanation without internal technical detail.

### BeachOS Support

BeachOS enforces the one-active-Customer-Group rule from DEC-011, treats Customer Group as the primary operational entity under DEC-031, applies Capability-based authorization under DEC-038, and preserves the Responsible Waiter-only opening rule under DEC-035. Under DEC-033, opening alone does not create a Bill unless an approved Financial Obligation exists. Under DEC-034, Beach Sunbed opening creates the immediate approved obligation and Bill. The action must be safely recorded before success is confirmed.

# 5. Operational Rules

1. **The Venue must be officially open.** Guest Arrival cannot proceed into seating or Customer Group opening before the daily Venue opening defined by DEC-039.
2. **Every mandatory Operational Department must have been READY at Venue opening.** The Host does not bypass or personally replace department readiness.
3. **One active Customer Group per Service Location.** An occupied location cannot receive another active Customer Group (DEC-011).
4. **Availability must come from operational truth.** Physical appearance alone is not enough when the current Service Location state says otherwise.
5. **Customer Group is the primary operational entity.** Arrival does not introduce a competing Visit or guest-account concept (DEC-031).
6. **The Host does not open the Customer Group.** Only the Responsible Waiter intentionally starts official service (DEC-035).
7. **Role does not imply authority.** The Responsible Waiter also requires Open Customer Group, and every protected action must satisfy its business rules (DEC-038).
8. **Identification is not action.** NFC or manual lookup identifies the Service Location only; it does not create a Customer Group, Bill, charge, Order, transfer, or Payment (DEC-030).
9. **Manual identification must remain available.** NFC failure must never be the only reason normal arrival cannot continue when identity can be verified safely (DEC-030).
10. **A Customer Group may exist without a Bill.** The Bill is created only when the first approved Financial Obligation exists (DEC-033).
11. **Beach Sunbed opening creates the approved immediate obligation.** The Responsible Waiter reviews and confirms it as part of intentional opening (DEC-034).
12. **Seating is not a financial action.** The Host’s welcome, proposal, escort, or location identification creates no Financial Obligation.
13. **No false success.** Customer Group opening is complete only after it is safely accepted and recorded.
14. **No unnecessary personal information.** Arrival requires only the information needed for safe, appropriate service.
15. **Uncertainty must remain visible.** A Host must not present an estimate, assumed location state, or expected handoff as a confirmed fact.
16. **Human hospitality remains human.** BeachOS supports information and rules; the Host decides how to welcome, communicate, and coordinate within those rules.
17. **Waiting remains manual until approved otherwise.** This workflow creates no digital waiting-list or reservation requirement.
18. **VIP recognition remains human and controlled.** BeachOS must not infer VIP status or create unapproved priority.

# 6. Edge Cases

## Venue full

### Situation

No suitable Service Location is currently confirmed available.

### Operational behaviour

The Host explains the situation honestly, offers the approved manual waiting process where one exists, and provides an estimate only when supported by current human knowledge. The Host does not promise a location, create an unofficial reservation, or treat a soon-to-be-free location as available.

If the guests wait, the Host maintains fair manual order and keeps them informed when the situation changes materially. If they leave, the Host acknowledges the decision respectfully.

### BeachOS boundary

BeachOS may provide current Service Location state. Digital waiting lists, queue sequencing, predicted release times, and automated priority are not approved.

## Family with children

### Situation

A family arrives with children or states practical family needs.

### Operational behaviour

The Host asks what the family needs rather than assuming. The Host considers safety, shade, movement, comfort, proximity, and the family’s stated preferences when choosing among confirmed available options.

If suitability, capacity, or safety is uncertain, the Host asks the responsible leader before making a promise.

### BeachOS boundary

BeachOS may provide approved location facts. Family profiling, automatic suitability matching, and child-related recommendations are future intelligence.

## Guest changes mind

### Situation

The guests reject the proposed location or change their preference before the Customer Group is opened.

### Operational behaviour

The Host returns to needs assessment or availability check without treating the change as a failure. The Host confirms whether another suitable location is available and explains any waiting consequence.

Because the Customer Group has not opened, this is not a Customer Group transfer. Once the Responsible Waiter has officially opened the group, any move must follow the approved transfer workflow and rules.

### BeachOS boundary

BeachOS provides current availability. It must not create a transfer for a party that does not yet have an active Customer Group.

## VIP arrival

### Situation

The Host recognizes the guest or receives a current approved instruction from the Manager.

### Operational behaviour

The Host provides discreet, thoughtful recognition while preserving safety, availability, privacy, and fairness. Any exception involving priority, price, an unavailable location, or another guest is escalated to the Manager.

### BeachOS boundary

BeachOS has no approved VIP profile, recognition, ranking, or priority feature. It must not infer VIP status.

## Wrong availability

### Situation

The central operational state and the physical condition of a Service Location do not match.

### Operational behaviour

The Host stops the seating commitment, apologizes for the uncertainty, and does not choose which version of reality to ignore. The Host contacts the responsible person, establishes the actual Customer Group or location condition, and ensures the source business action is corrected through an authorized process.

The Host offers another confirmed option or returns to waiting while the mismatch is resolved.

### BeachOS boundary

BeachOS preserves central operational truth and relevant history. The Host must not directly rewrite sensitive history merely to make the state match the desired seating outcome.

## Duplicate seating attempt

### Situation

Two Hosts or Staff Members attempt to seat or open different parties at the same Service Location.

### Operational behaviour

Both attempts pause. The team establishes which party has the valid confirmed operational claim according to the approved process. No one should pressure the Responsible Waiter to create a second Customer Group or use another location identity.

The affected guests receive a calm explanation and a new confirmed option where necessary.

### BeachOS boundary

BeachOS must enforce at most one active Customer Group per Service Location and refuse a conflicting opening. A refused attempt does not change the existing Customer Group or location state.

## Service Location becomes unavailable during escort

### Situation

The selected Service Location becomes occupied, unsafe, blocked, or otherwise unavailable before official Customer Group opening.

### Operational behaviour

The Host stops, explains that the situation changed, and returns to availability check. The Host proposes another confirmed suitable option or offers the approved manual waiting process.

No transfer occurs because no Customer Group has yet been opened. The Host does not continue merely to avoid embarrassment.

### BeachOS boundary

BeachOS can provide the changed current state. It does not decide which alternative best serves the guests.

## NFC identification fails

### Situation

The physical NFC tag is unavailable, damaged, unsupported, or does not resolve reliably.

### Operational behaviour

The Host uses the visible Service Location name or number for manual lookup. If identity remains uncertain, the Host stops and escalates rather than guessing.

### BeachOS boundary

Manual lookup must reach the same stable Service Location Digital Identity. Tag failure creates no new Service Location and changes no history (DEC-030).

## Responsible Waiter unavailable

### Situation

The party is seated, but no eligible Responsible Waiter can accept responsibility immediately.

### Operational behaviour

The Host remains accountable for coordinating the handoff, informs the guests honestly, and contacts the relevant service leader. The Host does not open the Customer Group or assign responsibility informally.

If responsibility cannot be established, the Venue must resolve staffing or offer another honest operational response before official service begins.

### BeachOS boundary

BeachOS can provide current responsibility and Capability context where approved. Automatic waiter assignment and workload-based selection are future intelligence.

# 7. KPIs

KPIs support coaching and workflow improvement. They must not encourage rushed greetings, false availability, unsafe seating, hidden waiting, or premature Customer Group opening.

## Greeting Time

**Definition:** Time from observable guest entry to the first meaningful acknowledgment by the Host or assigned arrival responsibility.

**What it helps reveal:** Whether guests are being noticed promptly and whether arrival coverage is sufficient.

**Interpretation rule:** A greeting must remain human and genuine. Faster is not better if the guest feels dismissed.

**Current measurement boundary:** Automatic guest-entry detection is not approved. Use direct observation or controlled operational sampling until a future method is approved.

## Arrival-to-Seating Time

**Definition:** Time from observable guest entry until the party reaches the accepted Service Location.

**What it helps reveal:** Availability clarity, Host coordination, walking impact, and avoidable delay in the seating process.

**Interpretation rule:** Separate immediate seating from time spent in the waiting process. Accessibility needs and thoughtful guest choice may appropriately increase time.

**Current measurement boundary:** A formal digital arrival or waiting process is not approved. Reliable automation requires future decisions.

## Arrival-to-Customer Group Opening

**Definition:** Time from observable guest entry until the Responsible Waiter’s Customer Group Opened event.

**What it helps reveal:** The complete arrival, seating, and service-handoff performance.

**Interpretation rule:** Never reward opening before the correct party, Service Location, Responsible Waiter, Capability, and immediate Financial Obligation are confirmed.

**Current measurement boundary:** Customer Group opening time is an accepted business fact. Arrival time requires an approved reliable source before automatic calculation.

## Waiting Percentage

**Definition:** Percentage of arriving parties that enter the approved waiting process rather than being seated immediately.

**What it helps reveal:** Demand pressure, availability constraints, layout suitability, and the need for clearer guest communication.

**Interpretation rule:** A high percentage is not automatically a Host failure. Capacity, weather, operating configuration, and guest preferences must be considered.

**Current measurement boundary:** Waiting-list tracking is not an accepted BeachOS feature and must remain manual until separately approved.

## Abandoned Guests

**Definition:** Number or percentage of arriving parties that leave before seating or before completing an offered waiting process.

**What it helps reveal:** Excessive delay, unclear communication, unsuitable availability, or a guest’s independent choice.

**Interpretation rule:** The reason matters. Do not assume that every departure could or should have been prevented.

**Current measurement boundary:** Recording guest departure from a waiting process requires an approved operational definition and privacy-conscious method.

## Availability Mismatch Rate

**Definition:** Frequency with which Service Location state used during arrival does not match the confirmed physical operation.

**What it helps reveal:** Weak handoffs, delayed business actions, incorrect location use, or trust problems in the operational mirror.

**Interpretation rule:** Investigate workflow and system conditions before assigning individual blame.

## Seating-to-Opening Time

**Definition:** Time from arrival at the accepted Service Location until the Responsible Waiter officially opens the Customer Group.

**What it helps reveal:** Whether service handoff is clear and timely.

**Interpretation rule:** The Customer Group must not open until the Responsible Waiter has accepted responsibility and all business rules are satisfied.

## Successful First-Time Identification

**Definition:** Percentage of Service Location identifications that resolve immediately to the correct visible location without requiring correction.

**What it helps reveal:** Physical marker quality, visible naming, staff familiarity, and manual fallback effectiveness.

**Interpretation rule:** NFC failure must not block service when safe manual identification is available.

## KPI use rules

- Interpret measures with volume, staffing, weather, layout, distance, accessibility, and guest choice.
- Use trends for coaching and operational improvement.
- Do not reward hidden waiting, rushed guests, false availability, or premature opening.
- Do not create personal surveillance to measure a process.
- Do not automate a KPI until its business start point, end point, exclusions, and privacy conditions are approved.

# 8. Common Mistakes

## Failing to acknowledge the guest

**Operational mistake:** The party enters but receives no sign that anyone has noticed.

**Why it matters:** Uncertainty begins before the conversation starts.

**BeachOS reduction:** BeachOS does not currently detect arrivals. Staffing, positioning, direct observation, and coaching remain the primary controls. Future arrival assistance requires approval.

## Starting with administration instead of hospitality

**Operational mistake:** The Host immediately asks several questions or explains rules without first welcoming the party.

**Why it matters:** The guest feels processed rather than received.

**BeachOS reduction:** BeachOS should not force unnecessary data entry before the Host can greet and understand the guests.

## Assuming guest needs

**Operational mistake:** The Host decides what a family, familiar guest, older guest, or disabled guest needs without asking.

**Why it matters:** Assumptions create poor seating and disrespect personal choice.

**BeachOS reduction:** The software provides approved location facts, not conclusions about the guest. Human conversation remains necessary.

## Promising before checking availability

**Operational mistake:** The Host promises a specific Service Location based on memory or appearance.

**Why it matters:** The promise may conflict with an active Customer Group, unavailable state, or another arrival.

**BeachOS reduction:** Current central Service Location state supports the availability check and the one-active-group invariant.

## Treating an empty-looking location as available

**Operational mistake:** Physical appearance is used as the only source of truth.

**Why it matters:** The location may belong to an active Customer Group or be intentionally unavailable.

**BeachOS reduction:** BeachOS provides current operational state. Mismatches must be investigated, not ignored.

## Overpromising waiting time

**Operational mistake:** A hopeful estimate is presented as a guarantee.

**Why it matters:** A later delay damages trust more than honest uncertainty.

**BeachOS reduction:** BeachOS currently provides availability, not approved waiting-time prediction. The Host must communicate uncertainty.

## Losing the guest during escort

**Operational mistake:** The Host walks too quickly, stops attending to the party, or fails to notice a changed physical condition.

**Why it matters:** The welcome becomes an impersonal transfer.

**BeachOS reduction:** This remains a human practice. Route and movement automation are not approved.

## Trusting NFC without checking physical identity

**Operational mistake:** The Host accepts a resolved location without comparing it with the visible name or number.

**Why it matters:** A moved or incorrect tag could direct the operation to the wrong context.

**BeachOS reduction:** Stable identity and manual lookup support verification. A tag never becomes the Service Location itself.

## Treating identification as authorization

**Operational mistake:** The Host expects NFC or manual lookup to open service or create a charge.

**Why it matters:** It bypasses intentional responsibility and may create unintended financial consequences.

**BeachOS reduction:** DEC-030 requires identification to perform no operational or financial action.

## Leaving before waiter handoff

**Operational mistake:** The Host escorts the guests and walks away before service responsibility is clear.

**Why it matters:** The guests are seated but operationally unowned.

**BeachOS reduction:** Responsible Waiter context can support clarity, but direct human acknowledgment remains necessary.

## Host opens the Customer Group

**Operational mistake:** The Host attempts to complete official service opening for convenience.

**Why it matters:** It violates the approved responsibility boundary and may create a Beach Sunbed Financial Obligation through the wrong person.

**BeachOS reduction:** DEC-035 and Capability rules must prevent the Host from performing the Responsible Waiter’s opening action.

## Opening twice after uncertainty

**Operational mistake:** A repeated attempt creates or appears to create a second Customer Group.

**Why it matters:** Duplicate occupancy and financial state become possible.

**BeachOS reduction:** The system must safely reject a conflicting or duplicate opening and confirm success only after durable acceptance.

## Hiding a mismatch to protect appearance

**Operational mistake:** The Host ignores wrong availability or responsibility because correcting it may delay the guest.

**Why it matters:** The digital operational mirror becomes untrustworthy.

**BeachOS reduction:** Preserve current state and relevant history; corrections must follow approved, traceable processes.

# 9. Implementation Notes

## Already implementable

The following operational support is grounded in accepted decisions and requirements:

- verification that the Venue has officially opened for the Business Date under DEC-039;
- current Service Location identity and availability from the central source of truth;
- one active Customer Group per Service Location under DEC-011;
- physical-to-digital Service Location identification through NFC;
- manual identification by visible name or number when NFC is unavailable;
- the rule that identification performs no operational or financial action under DEC-030;
- current Area and Zone context without allowing Zone membership to change Service Location identity;
- Responsible Waiter visibility and responsibility;
- Capability-based authorization under DEC-038;
- prevention of Host-created Customer Groups under DEC-035;
- intentional Customer Group opening by the Responsible Waiter;
- Customer Group as the primary operational entity under DEC-031;
- a Customer Group existing without a Bill where no Financial Obligation exists under DEC-033;
- immediate approved Beach Sunbed Financial Obligation and Bill creation under DEC-034;
- rejection of opening when the Service Location is occupied or unavailable;
- safe confirmation only after Customer Group opening has been accepted and recorded;
- permanent operational and financial history for sensitive actions.

These accepted foundations support the final operational steps. Human greeting, needs assessment, suitability judgment, escort, and direct handoff can be trained immediately without new software.

“Already implementable” does not authorize application development. It means the business behaviour is sufficiently grounded for later design and acceptance work. Existing unresolved transfer, charging-exception, Capability-governance, and completion questions remain unresolved.

## Future Operational Intelligence

The following are outside current accepted BeachOS behaviour and require separate Product Owner decisions:

- automatic guest-entry detection;
- digital arrival registration;
- digital waiting-list management;
- queue sequencing or priority;
- waiting-time or Service Location release prediction;
- reservations or pre-arrival matching;
- family, accessibility, or preference profiles;
- automatic Service Location suitability recommendations;
- VIP profiles, recognition, ranking, or priority;
- facial recognition or identity inference;
- automatic Responsible Waiter assignment;
- workload-based waiter recommendations;
- automated handoff confirmation;
- crowd-aware or weather-aware escort guidance;
- automated availability-mismatch resolution;
- guest abandonment detection;
- automated guest satisfaction inference;
- arrival performance scoring;
- AI-generated seating or hospitality decisions.

Any future intelligence must:

- solve a verified operational problem;
- remain useful to hospitality rather than administration alone;
- use the minimum necessary information;
- explain its basis and uncertainty;
- preserve human judgment;
- respect Role, Capability, privacy, and business-rule boundaries;
- keep manual fallback available where continuity requires it;
- receive Product Owner approval and an accepted Decision when material.
