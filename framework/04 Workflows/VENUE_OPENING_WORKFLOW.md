# Venue Opening Workflow

## Status

Accepted.

Related decision: DEC-039.

## Purpose

The Venue Opening Workflow confirms that the operation is ready before guests are served.

It creates one shared view of readiness without asking the Host to personally inspect every department. Each Operational Department owns its declaration. The Host coordinates the complete process and performs the intentional opening action only when the Venue satisfies the approved readiness condition.

## Operational Outcome

The workflow succeeds when:

- every mandatory Operational Department has declared READY;
- the Host can understand the complete readiness state;
- the Host intentionally opens the Venue while holding the required Capability;
- the official operational start of the Business Date is clear;
- guest service becomes available only after opening;
- a permanent Opening Report preserves the conditions of opening.

## Roles and Responsibilities

### Operational Department responsible person

For the current opening, each mandatory Operational Department has one responsible person.

That person:

- verifies the readiness of their own department;
- declares READY, NOT READY, or BLOCKED;
- provides a reason when declaring NOT READY or BLOCKED;
- adds useful comments where context will help the Host or later review;
- updates the declaration when the department’s real condition changes;
- does not declare READY merely to avoid delaying the opening.

Initial department examples are:

- Kitchen;
- Bar;
- Beach Operations;
- Cleaning;
- Maintenance;
- Cashier;
- Reception, where applicable.

A Venue may require a different set of departments through an approved operating rule.

### Host

The Host coordinates the opening process.

The Host:

- sees the current declaration from every mandatory department;
- follows up on missing declarations, delays, and blocking issues;
- helps the responsible people coordinate dependencies;
- makes sure comments and reasons are understandable;
- does not personally replace each department’s verification;
- opens the Venue only when all mandatory departments are READY and the Host holds the required Capability.

Opening the Venue does not authorize the Host to open a Customer Group. The existing responsible-service rule remains separate.

### Manager

Where the Venue assigns this responsibility, a Manager supports escalation and removal of blockers.

The Manager does not mark a department READY merely to bypass the workflow. Any approved substitution, exception, or change of responsible person must remain clear and traceable.

## Readiness Statuses

### READY

The department’s responsible person confirms that the department can perform its required opening responsibilities.

READY is a positive declaration of current operational readiness. It is not an estimate and must not hide a known blocking condition.

### NOT READY

The department is not yet ready but is expected to become ready through continuing preparation.

A reason is mandatory. The responsible person should provide enough context for coordination, including the expected next action when known.

Examples:

- Beach cleaning is still in progress.
- Cashier preparation is incomplete.
- Kitchen opening checks have not finished.

### BLOCKED

The department cannot become ready until an issue, dependency, or decision is resolved.

A reason is mandatory. The declaration should identify the blocker clearly enough for the Host and appropriate leader to coordinate a response.

Examples:

- Ice machine failure.
- Kitchen equipment issue.
- Required operating system unavailable.
- Safety issue awaiting Maintenance.

BLOCKED describes the operating condition. It is not a judgment about the person declaring it.

## Opening Sequence

### 1. Establish the opening scope

The Venue identifies the Business Date, the mandatory Operational Departments, and the responsible person for each department.

Any missing responsibility must be resolved before opening can complete.

### 2. Departments prepare

Each responsible person prepares and verifies their own department according to its approved standards and playbooks.

The Host monitors progress and coordinates dependencies but does not take ownership of every verification.

### 3. Departments declare readiness

Each mandatory department declares one current status:

- READY;
- NOT READY;
- BLOCKED.

NOT READY and BLOCKED require a reason. Comments may provide additional useful context.

### 4. The Host coordinates unresolved readiness

The Host uses the Opening Dashboard to identify:

- missing declarations;
- NOT READY departments;
- BLOCKED departments;
- delays;
- issues affecting more than one department;
- comments or reasons requiring follow-up.

The Host brings the relevant people together, escalates where appropriate, and keeps the shared opening picture clear.

### 5. Departments update their own status

When a real condition changes, the responsible person updates the department declaration.

A resolved blocker does not automatically make the department READY. The responsible person must verify readiness and intentionally declare READY.

### 6. The Venue becomes eligible to open

OPEN VENUE becomes available only when every mandatory department is READY.

An optional department does not block opening unless an approved rule makes it mandatory for that Business Date.

### 7. The Host opens the Venue

The Host reviews the complete readiness picture and intentionally executes OPEN VENUE while holding the required Capability.

This action:

- creates the official operational start of the Business Date;
- enables guest arrivals;
- allows new Customer Groups under their existing opening rules;
- allows Orders under their existing rules;
- starts operational timers;
- begins collection of the day’s dashboard measures;
- creates the official Opening Report.

It does not create a Customer Group, Bill, Financial Obligation, Order, transfer, or Payment.

### 8. Preserve the Opening Report

The permanent Opening Report contains:

- Venue and Business Date;
- opening time;
- Host;
- every mandatory department and its readiness at opening;
- delays;
- blocking issues;
- reasons;
- comments.

The report becomes part of daily operational history and must remain understandable after the opening moment has passed.

## Opening Dashboard

The Opening Dashboard is the Host’s real-time operational view of the workflow.

It should make clear:

- which departments are mandatory;
- who is responsible for each declaration;
- the current status of each department;
- which declarations are missing;
- why a department is NOT READY or BLOCKED;
- current comments, delays, and blocking issues;
- whether the Venue is eligible to open.

The dashboard supports coordination. It does not transfer verification responsibility from departments to the Host and does not override Capability or business rules.

## Forbidden Outcomes

The workflow must never allow:

- the Venue to open while a mandatory department is NOT READY, BLOCKED, or missing a declaration;
- NOT READY or BLOCKED without a reason;
- the Host to be shown as the verifier of work declared by another department;
- the Host Role alone to authorize OPEN VENUE;
- an automatic opening caused only by the passage of time;
- guest arrivals, new Customer Groups, or Orders before daily opening;
- OPEN VENUE to create a Customer Group or any financial action;
- the Opening Report to be silently deleted or rewritten;
- operational pressure to turn a false READY declaration into acceptable practice.

## Exceptions and Recovery

DEC-039 defines no exception that permits opening while a mandatory department is not READY.

If business experience later shows that a controlled exception is necessary, the owner must approve a new decision defining:

- who may authorize it;
- which conditions are eligible;
- which safeguards are mandatory;
- what guests and staff may experience;
- how the exception appears in the Opening Report and Audit Trail.

Until then, the Venue remains closed.

If readiness changes after opening, the issue belongs to the appropriate live-operations or incident Playbook. The historical Opening Report must continue to reflect the confirmed state at the moment of opening.

## Operational Review

Managers should use Opening Reports to understand recurring delays, blockers, dependencies, and opportunities for coaching.

The report should support improvement rather than blame. A delayed opening may reveal equipment, staffing, sequencing, knowledge, or coordination problems that the business should address.

Review should consider:

- whether mandatory departments were appropriate;
- whether responsibility was clear;
- whether reasons were useful;
- whether blockers repeated;
- whether the Host could coordinate without unnecessary checking;
- whether opening occurred only after genuine readiness;
- how the opening affected guest experience, team performance, and owner confidence.
