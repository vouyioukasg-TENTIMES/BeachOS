# Emerald Management System — Product Vision

## 1. Mission

Emerald Management System exists to make hospitality service calmer, faster, and more accountable.

Our mission is to give every member of a busy venue team one clear view of customers, locations, orders, preparation, bills, and payments—so staff can focus on hospitality instead of chasing information.

## 2. The problem we solve

Beach bars and similar venues are harder to operate than ordinary table-service businesses. Customers may be spread across beach rows, pool areas, and upper tables. Staff travel long distances, orders go to different preparation points, groups move, and sunbed pricing can depend on location.

When teams rely on memory, paper, verbal messages, or disconnected tools, predictable problems appear:

- bar or kitchen items are delayed, duplicated, or lost;
- nobody is certain which waiter owns a sunbed set;
- running bills are difficult to check;
- moving a group creates confusion about its location and balance;
- cashiers see problems only when the customer is ready to pay;
- owners lack a reliable view of sales, corrections, and daily operations;
- staff spend time coordinating the system instead of serving guests.

Emerald solves this by keeping the full service journey connected, from opening a customer group to closing its bill.

## 3. Target customers

### First customer

The first customer is Emerald, a beach bar in Loutraki, Greece. The product will be shaped by its real staff, venue layout, seasonal pressure, and daily operating rules.

### Primary future customers

- independent beach bars and organised beaches;
- pool clubs and resorts with distributed service areas;
- seasonal hospitality venues with many temporary staff;
- venues combining sunbeds, tables, bars, and kitchens;
- small hospitality groups that need one shared operating view across several locations.

### People we serve inside each business

- waiters who need fast, dependable order taking;
- bar and kitchen teams who need clear preparation queues;
- cashiers who need accurate, visible bills;
- managers who need control without constant intervention;
- owners who need trustworthy performance and financial insight.

The guests are indirect users: they benefit through quicker service, fewer mistakes, and a smoother payment experience.

## 4. Market opportunity

Many hospitality systems begin with indoor restaurant tables and adapt beach or pool operations as an afterthought. Distributed venues have different needs: responsibility by location, row-based charges, long walking distances, changing customer positions, multiple preparation areas, outdoor devices, seasonal workers, and unreliable connectivity.

This creates an opportunity for a focused product that understands these operations from the beginning. Greece provides a strong starting environment because beach and seasonal hospitality are important parts of the visitor economy. Over time, the same operating model can serve similar Mediterranean and resort markets.

The opportunity is not to become every type of business software immediately. It is to become exceptionally trusted at the live service layer, then expand carefully into the back-office and multi-venue needs customers repeatedly ask for.

Any investment or expansion decision should be supported by customer research, pilot results, willingness to pay, and current market analysis rather than this vision alone.

## 5. Why this product is different

### Built around a customer group, not only a table

A group can belong to a sunbed set, pool position, or upper table and can move without losing its service history or bill.

### Clear human responsibility

The product makes the responsible waiter visible, while preserving teamwork and an accountable history of changes.

### Beach and pool rules are first-class

Rows, sunbed sets, location-based charges, and complete transfers are core ideas rather than awkward additions to a restaurant system.

### One flow from order to payment

Waiters, bar staff, kitchen staff, cashiers, and managers work from the same live information. An order is not finished when it leaves the waiter’s phone; it remains connected through preparation, delivery, billing, and closing.

### Designed for real working conditions

Emerald prioritises sunlight readability, touch use, mobile movement, intermittent connections, busy shifts, and quick staff learning.

### Trust over feature count

The product aims to do critical work reliably and transparently. It will not hide uncertainty, silently alter financial history, or add complexity simply to match a competitor’s checklist.

### Shaped with operators

Decisions will be tested with the people doing the work. The first venue is a learning partner, not merely a place to install software.

## 6. Product values

### Hospitality first

Technology should create more time and attention for guests.

### Clarity

Every user should understand what is happening, what needs action, and what has been completed.

### Trust

Orders, bills, payments, and history must be dependable and explainable.

### Speed with care

Common work should be fast, but never at the cost of correctness.

### Respect for staff

The product should reduce stress, avoid blame-based design, and support people during demanding shifts.

### Accountability

Responsibility and sensitive changes should be visible without turning the workplace into surveillance.

### Simplicity

We solve the real problem with the fewest concepts and steps that safely work.

### Learning

We listen to staff, observe actual operation, measure outcomes, and change our assumptions when evidence disagrees.

### Inclusion

The product should work across roles, devices, languages, and different levels of technical confidence.

### Sustainable growth

We expand only when we can preserve service quality, customer trust, and operational support.

## 7. Long-term roadmap

The roadmap describes direction, not promised dates. Each stage depends on evidence and successful adoption of the previous one.

### Horizon 1 — Prove the core at Emerald

- connect venue locations, staff responsibility, orders, bar and kitchen preparation, running bills, sunbed charges, transfers, payments, and closing;
- make the system dependable on phones, tablets, and desktop computers;
- train staff and prove it during real shifts;
- establish accurate records, support, backup, and recovery;
- learn which workflows truly matter and remove avoidable friction.

### Horizon 2 — Strengthen daily operations

- improve shift handovers, exception handling, and manager controls;
- add the reports and exports owners and accountants actually use;
- support agreed printing, payment, fiscal, and accounting connections;
- improve menu, pricing, promotion, tip, and payment flexibility;
- deepen preparation tracking and service-speed insight;
- strengthen operation under weak connectivity where evidence requires it.

### Horizon 3 — Expand the venue platform

- add reservations, deposits, advanced venue layouts, and customer requests;
- support stock, recipes, purchasing, suppliers, and wastage;
- introduce carefully designed customer-facing ordering, payment, and loyalty options;
- add planning and forecasting that turns operating data into useful decisions;
- provide integrations with the tools customers already depend on.

### Horizon 4 — Serve multiple venues

- make onboarding and configuration repeatable for other beach bars and resort venues;
- support hospitality groups with shared oversight and local control;
- provide cross-venue reporting, standard menus, capability-based authorization, and operational comparison;
- establish dependable training, migration, support, and partner processes.

### Horizon 5 — Become a focused hospitality operating platform

- expand into suitable Mediterranean and resort markets;
- support local languages, currencies, taxes, and country-specific obligations;
- offer a trusted integration platform for approved partners;
- use responsible automation and AI to help predict demand, identify unusual activity, simplify setup, and assist staff—while keeping people in control.

## 8. Success metrics

Success must combine customer outcomes, staff experience, business value, reliability, and sustainable growth. Baselines should be measured before launch, then targets agreed for each stage.

### Service outcomes

- median time from order submission to preparation acceptance and readiness;
- percentage of orders delivered without missing, duplicated, or wrongly routed items;
- time from payment request to bill closure;
- number of order and bill corrections per 100 customer groups;
- number of unresolved responsibility or location disputes.

### Staff outcomes

- time required to train a new waiter on core tasks;
- percentage of staff who can complete core workflows without assistance;
- staff-rated ease, clarity, and confidence during busy shifts;
- number and severity of support requests per shift;
- reduction in verbal checking and repeated data entry.

### Owner outcomes

- difference between expected and recorded daily payments;
- percentage of open bills clearly assigned and visible;
- time required for end-of-day review;
- visibility of cancellations, discounts, transfers, and corrections;
- active use of reports that lead to an operating decision.

### Product health

- availability during operating hours;
- percentage of order, transfer, and payment actions completed without system error;
- time to recover from incidents;
- successful backup and restoration checks;
- adoption and continued use by each staff role.

### Business growth

- pilot venues that become continuing customers;
- customer retention and renewal;
- customer recommendation and satisfaction;
- time and effort needed to launch a new venue;
- recurring revenue compared with the cost of support and operation;
- proportion of growth coming from referrals and proven customer value.

Metrics must not reward harmful shortcuts. For example, staff should never feel pressure to close bills early merely to improve a speed measure.

## 9. Product promise

Emerald Management System promises to make the state of service visible, keep the customer journey connected, and protect the accuracy of operational and financial records.

We will build from real venue needs, keep staff work simple, and grow only in ways that preserve trust. The product should become something a team can rely on without thinking about the technology behind it—the quiet shared system that helps a difficult shift run well.
